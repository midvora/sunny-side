Texas summers are hard on heavy-duty equipment. Heat stresses tires, cooling systems and batteries all at once, and a small problem in spring can become a roadside call by July. This checklist covers the items that matter most.

## Tires

- **Check pressure when the tires are cold.** Pressure climbs as tires heat up on the road, so a cold reading is the dependable one.
- **Don't run underinflated.** Low pressure makes a tire flex more, which builds heat and raises the risk of a blowout.
- **Look for cuts, bulges and uneven wear.** Do it at every stop. A tire that has been running hot or low deserves a closer look before the next trip.
- **Check trailer tires too.** They're easy to forget and just as exposed.

## Cooling system

- **Coolant level and condition.** Check the overflow tank when the engine is cool.
- **Hoses and clamps.** Look for cracks, swelling and seepage.
- **Belts.** Check for glazing, cracking and proper tension.
- **Radiator and charge-air cooler.** Keep the fins free of bugs, dirt and debris so air can flow through.
- **Fan operation.** Make sure it engages when it should.

## Batteries and charging

Heat shortens battery life and can accelerate corrosion. Check terminals for corrosion, make sure connections are tight and have older batteries tested before the heat arrives rather than after.

## Fluids

- **Engine oil level.** Engines run hotter in summer, so check regularly.
- **DEF.** Store diesel exhaust fluid out of direct sun when you can.
- **Hub oil on trailers.** Check wheel-end oil levels, and look for leaks at the hub caps.

## Air conditioning

A working A/C isn't a luxury on a long Texas day. It affects alertness and safety. If it's weak or noisy, get it looked at before it quits.

## During the trip

- Take a quick walk-around at fuel stops and check tires with your eyes and your hand on the sidewall. A tire running much hotter than the rest is a warning sign.
- Watch your temperature gauge on grades and in traffic.
- If a warning light comes on, find a safe place to stop rather than pushing on.

## When something goes wrong

If you do break down in the heat, stay safe, stay hydrated and get help rolling. See our [first ten minutes checklist](/resources/broken-down-first-10-minutes/) and [roadside assistance](/roadside-assistance/).

A [preventive maintenance service](/services/preventive-maintenance/) before the hottest months is the best way to find tired hoses, weak batteries and low fluids while it's still cheap and convenient to fix them.
