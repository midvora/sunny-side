If you run a commercial motor vehicle, the annual inspection is one of those jobs that's easy to put off and expensive to ignore. Here's what it is, what it covers and how to walk in prepared.

## What the rule says

Under 49 CFR 396.17, every commercial motor vehicle must be inspected at least once every 12 months. The inspection has to cover, at a minimum, the components listed in Appendix A to Part 396. The motor carrier is responsible for making sure it happens.

An annual inspection is not the same as a roadside inspection. A roadside inspection is done by an enforcement officer at the side of the road. The annual inspection is a periodic, scheduled inspection of the vehicle itself.

## What gets checked

Appendix A groups the minimum inspection standards by system. The categories include:

- **Brake system**, including service brakes, parking brakes and the air system
- **Coupling devices**, such as fifth wheels and pintle hooks
- **Exhaust system**
- **Fuel system**
- **Lighting devices**
- **Safe loading**
- **Steering mechanism**
- **Suspension**
- **Frame**
- **Tires**
- **Wheels and rims**
- **Windshield glazing and windshield wipers**
- **Rear impact guard** on trailers

For the exact standards under each heading, read Appendix A to Part 396 on the eCFR. A good shop will walk through each one and explain anything that doesn't pass.

## How to prepare

You don't need to fix everything before you arrive, but a few minutes of preparation saves time and surprises.

1. **Do a walk-around.** Check lights, reflectors and markers, tires, wheels and visible leaks.
2. **Check your brakes and air system.** Listen for air leaks, watch your air build-up and note anything that feels different.
3. **Bring your paperwork.** Any prior inspection reports and any roadside inspection reports from the past year.
4. **Write down what you've noticed.** Noises, pulling, warning lights and the dates they started.
5. **Make time.** A thorough inspection takes longer than a quick look. Call ahead so we can plan around your schedule.

## After the inspection

You'll get a report listing what passed and what needs attention. Items that don't meet the standard should be repaired, and the repairs documented. Keep the inspection report with your maintenance records as the rules require.

## Don't wait for the deadline

If your 12-month date is close, call early. Shops fill up near deadlines, and fixing a problem found at inspection can take parts and time.
