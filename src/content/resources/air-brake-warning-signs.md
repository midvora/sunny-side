Air brakes are reliable when they're maintained, and unforgiving when they're not. The good news is that they usually give warnings first. Here are the ones to take seriously.

## How air brakes work, in one paragraph

A compressor fills air tanks. Pressing the brake pedal sends air to brake chambers, which push the brakes against the drums. Springs hold the parking brakes on until air pressure releases them. If the system loses air, the spring brakes are designed to apply. That is why a leak is never "just a leak."

## Warning signs

### The low-air warning comes on

Federal rules require a warning signal that comes on when air pressure in the service reservoir drops to 55 psi or below (49 CFR 393.51). If you see the light or hear the buzzer, don't keep driving as if nothing has changed. Find a safe place to stop and get it checked.

### You hear air hissing

Air leaking from a hose, fitting or brake chamber often shows up as a hiss when the truck is parked or when you press the pedal. A small leak now can become a big one on the road.

### Air pressure builds slowly

If it takes much longer than usual for the system to build pressure after starting, the compressor, air dryer or a leak could be the cause.

### The truck pulls to one side

Uneven braking from one side to the other can come from a stuck or out-of-adjustment brake, a contaminated lining or a failing chamber.

### The pedal feels soft or stopping distance grows

Brakes out of adjustment, worn linings or leaks can all make the truck take longer to stop.

### The ABS light stays on

The ABS warning light that doesn't go out after startup, or comes on while driving, points to a sensor, wiring or modulator issue that needs diagnosing.

### Heat, smell or smoke from a wheel

A wheel end that smells hot or smokes can mean dragging brakes or a failing bearing. Stop and let it cool before you diagnose it, and don't keep driving on it.

## What to do

- **Don't ignore it.** Brake problems don't improve with miles.
- **Stop in a safe place** if a warning comes on while you're driving.
- **Call for help.** If you're in the Garland area, our [roadside assistance](/roadside-assistance/) team can come to you, and many air leaks can be fixed on the spot.
- **Do your pre-trip.** Check air pressure build-up and the air leakage tests described in your CDL manual before every trip.
